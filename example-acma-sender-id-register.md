# Example: Regulatory/News Announcement Post
**Source:** blog.clicksend.com/acma-sender-id-register/ — "ACMA's new Sender ID Register has arrived. Here's what it means for your business."
**Author:** Anna Christensen | **Category:** SMS guides | **Length:** ~2 min read (shortest of the set)

## Content type
Timely regulatory news post — explains a compliance change and drives urgency to act.

## Structure pattern
1. **Shock-stat hook** — leads with a large, alarming number tied to the problem the regulation solves (scam losses)
2. **Villain/loophole framing** — explains the exploit that existed before the change, making the "why" visceral and easy to picture
3. **The change, stated plainly** — one or two sentences: what changed and the effective date
4. **"Why this matters" section** — regulator context, historical gap, supporting stat
5. **Before/after contrast** — two-item comparison of what happens with vs. without compliance (registered vs. "Unverified" label)
6. **Urgency section** — social proof stat (how many businesses still haven't acted) to create FOMO/urgency without being alarmist
7. **Action checklist** — short numbered/bulleted list of concrete next steps
8. **Repetition close** — restates the core stakes in a punchy, symmetric closing line ("Register now... Wait, and...")
9. **Support links** — low-key closing pointers to help docs/support rather than a hard sales CTA

## Tone
Urgent but not fear-mongering. Short sentences. Uses direct address and rhetorical framing ("Now's your time to move").

## Formatting devices
- Bolded section questions as headers ("Why this matters", "What this means for your messages")
- Short punchy closing lines with parallel structure
- Minimal images/tables — text-forward given the short length

## Useful for tool tailoring
Reuse for: compliance updates, regulatory changes, policy announcements, any "here's what changed and what to do about it" content. Notably the shortest/punchiest format in the set — good template when brevity matters.
