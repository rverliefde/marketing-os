# Example: Customer Case Study
**Source:** blog.clicksend.com/pulsemedica-no-shows-sms-api/ — "How PulseMedica reduced no-shows and strengthened patient security with ClickSend's API"
**Author:** Anna Christensen | **Category:** Customer Story | **Length:** ~4 min read

## Content type
Classic B2B case study: named customer, specific problem, specific solution, quoted results.

## Structure pattern
1. **Universal problem framing** — opens with the general stakes before naming the customer, so it lands for any reader in that industry
2. **Customer introduction** — who they are, their mission/promise to their own customers, and where the product fits into that promise
3. **"The cost of..." problem section** — direct customer quotes describing the specific failure of their previous solution
4. **Requirements section** — presented as "three non-negotiables," each bolded and explained, functioning as an implicit product-fit checklist
5. **Resolution statement** — short, direct: "X met all three," followed by a supporting quote
6. **"How they use it day-to-day"** — bolded use-case sub-headers, each with a short explanation of the specific workflow
7. **Results section** — quotes paired with concrete outcomes, organized by theme (operational, security)
8. **Forward-looking close** — brief note on scale/growth, ending on a broader industry statement that reinforces the stakes from the opening

## Tone
Third-person narrative with direct customer quotes woven in throughout — not just pulled out as isolated blockquotes. Quotes are integrated into the sentence flow.

## Formatting devices
- Bolded phrase leading into each use-case paragraph (acts as a mini sub-header without a full heading)
- No table this time — more narrative/prose-driven than the other examples
- Numbers/stats used to open sections (cost of no-shows) and close them (scale reached)

## Useful for tool tailoring
Reuse for: any customer success story, testimonial-driven content, social proof pages. Note the pattern of stating the general industry problem before narrowing to the specific customer — useful structural device to replicate.
