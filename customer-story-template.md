# Customer story template

---

## Before you start — writing tips

### Pre-writing

**Note down your key takeaway**
What do you want the audience to know (or think) once they've finished reading? A good customer story highlights "what's in it for me?" for the target audience.

**Pull out the best quotes and stats**
These are the proof points for your story. Keep them handy.

### After writing

**Edit ruthlessly**
Cut long sentences. Make sure each sentence has its own idea. Replace stuffy words with simple ones. Write in an active voice. Check your copy for readability with the Hemingway editor — aim for a score between 6–9.

**Make sure the reader gets it**
After reading this for the first time, could you confidently explain it to someone else? Will the reader relate to the story?

**Add rich media where you can**
Think about where you can add video, pictures or links to give the reader more context.

## Story structure

### Headline

This is the hook. Make it attention-grabbing and focused on customer results.

If you're stuck, try this formula:
> How [customer] did [result] with [product, feature or approach]

---

### Context

Explain who the customer is and what they do. Write about the company as if you were talking to a coworker about them — ditch the marketing speak, keep it human.

---

### [Creative subhead]

**Goal & obstacle**
What does the customer want to achieve and why — how does their goal change the world or their business? Then hit the obstacle: what's in the way of their goal? What's at stake? What needs to happen?

---

### [Creative subhead]

**Show, don't tell**
Get some visuals designed. Use a real SMS from the company or showcase some numbers or stats.

---

### [Creative subhead]

**Outcome**
Did they achieve their goal? How? What did they learn? What's next for the customer? What would they tell other companies who wanted to do the same thing?

---

### Quote

Finish on a customer quote. Include the person's name and their job title.

---

### CTA

Keep it short and sweet — 25–50 words.
