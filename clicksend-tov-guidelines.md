## What is tone of voice?

Every time a customer interacts with ClickSend, it's an opportunity to show off who we are — our personality, vision, mission and values.

Having a consistent, expert brand voice within the CPaaS world helps customers get to know ClickSend. And know what to expect from us. It's all part of building trust and great relationships with our customers.

Use these guidelines to:
- Learn our brand voice
- Write customer-centric copy
- Stay on-point with grammar rules and words we use

## Our four pillars

### Empathetic

We're always customer-first. We show our customers that we're tuned into them. Which means we talk less about ourselves and our products. And more about our customers.

We're always friendly, helpful and conversational. We turn features into benefits where possible. We use simple language that doesn't exclude anyone — including people from diverse groups.

**This means we:**
- Always think: what's in it for the customer? How can we help and what do we do for their business?
- Use the 'you' rule — we use more 'you' and less 'we' or 'our'
- Use positive and informal language that feels like a conversation
- Are honest and straightforward. No outlandish claims or statistics

---

### Smart

We're a team of smart people. But we don't flaunt our intelligence with big words. We make the complex simple instead. Just like our tools do.

We use clear, concise and relatable language — the kind of words our customers use themselves. We're positive, energising and encouraging, like a mentor who guides the way (without taking the wheel).

**This means we:**
- Cut the waffle, ClickSend jargon and corporate speak
- Rephrase sentences or choose simple synonyms to be as clear as possible
- Replace any long words with shorter ones, so it's easy to read
- Break up the copy with subheadings and bullet points so it's easy to read

---

### Bold

We're confident about our tools. And we want our customers to feel the same. All so they can self-serve, jump in and get sending.

That's why our comms inspire action and show what's possible — without overpromising. Think quiet confidence, rather than loud or boastful. We get straight to the point and don't waste words.

**This means we:**
- Avoid being vague. We use decisive language such as can, do and yes
- Write short and simple sentences. Pack a punch and get straight to the point
- Always write in the active voice, never passive
- Use strong verbs to inspire action and immediacy where possible

---

### A little weird

We're happy to be a little offbeat and eccentric. To think creatively and have some fun. That's how new ideas are born, after all.

We don't use tired clichés and same-same language. We think of new ways to explain tired old concepts. We like to make customers smile and be authentic.

We're not vanilla, boring or dull. But our humour is never silly, cringe or overwhelming. Think likeable-weird — not weird-weird.

**This means we:**
- Are never inappropriate, condescending or make jokes at the expense of others
- Don't force it. If a joke doesn't feel natural, don't make one
- Use modern, flexible and upbeat language — that's a little unexpected
- Aren't afraid to add some personality and write like you talk

## How we speak — 8 rules

**1. It's more about the customer, less about us**
Before you write — think: what's in it for the customer? Try to talk about benefits, outcomes and address their pain points. Rather than writing about how great we are.

**2. Delete extra words, always**
Re-read sentences after writing and see what words you can cut. Get rid of any extra words, jargon and get straight to the point. But be careful — never, ever sacrifice clarity for brevity.

**3. Axe the !**
Our copy is confident and should stand alone. If your sentence needs an exclamation mark, go back and rewrite it. Try to make the sentence bolder by using strong verbs and shortening where possible.

**4. Be a freak about structure**
Use subheadings, short paragraphs and bullet points. Make sure the content is well laid out so it's easier to read. Avoid complex multi-clause sentences or repeating yourself. Try to make sure there's only one idea in each sentence.

**5. We like features — but we like benefits too**
Turn features into benefits wherever you can. Write less: "Tier 1 direct routes." And more: "Text messages that always get delivered." Remember, our features set us apart too. Use features to back up benefits at key customer decision moments.

**6. No more same-same**
If you write something and it sounds like a cliché, it probably is. For example: "save time and money." Try to rewrite these tired old idioms and sentiments into something new. Don't be afraid to be a little weird.

**7. Give great directions**
Always provide a call to action. It's one way we help guide our customers, so they know what to do next. It's all part of being an empathetic brand, helping customers move along.

**8. Short paragraphs and sentences rule**
Read your sentences aloud. If you have to read it twice, it's too long. Always try to make your sentences shorter or split one sentence into two. The ideal standard for long-form content is no more than 20 words per sentence, five sentences per paragraph.

## How to adjust tone for customer service

**Check the temperature**
If you're handling a customer complaint, be empathetic first and foremost. If you're delivering good news, you can add a little boldness — and maybe even a sprinkle of weirdness.

**Be a good human**
Be friendly, helpful and conversational. Write like you talk and ditch the formalities. Use contractions (we're, don't) and personal pronouns (I, we, you).

Instead of: *"Hi Sir, how can ClickSend be of service to you today"*
Try: *"Hey, Sam. How can I help?"*

**They're busy. Get to it.**
When they're reaching out to customer support, they need help fast. Give them clear instructions. Use consistent subheadings and break up chunky content with bullet points.

## The finer details

### Brand name
It's always **ClickSend**. Never clicksend, Clicksend or Click Send.

### Sentence case
All headings use sentence case. The first word is capitalised and the rest of the sentence is lowercase. Proper nouns are always capitalised.

### Full stops
Headings don't use full stops — even if they are full sentences.

### Dates
- Most regions: `24 January 2022`
- US: `January 24, 2022`

### Numbers
- Spell out one to nine: one, two, three...
- Use numerals for 10 and above: 10, 25, 100
- Use numerals if it gives more impact in a heading: *7 common SMS slip ups*

### Times
Use `am` and `pm` without a space: `9am`, `9.30pm`. Use a dot for exact times. Always include the time zone when necessary.

### Buttons and CTAs
Think of the action you want the reader to take and summarise it in 2–3 words. Make sure your CTA explains what the user will get when clicked. Be as clear as possible.

### Em dashes
If using dashes to emphasise a point or in place of a semicolon, always use an em dash: **—**
Do not use an en dash (–) or a hyphen (-).

### Contractions
Always use contractions. It's not "we are" — it's "we're". Switch "do not" for "don't".

### URLs
Write URLs without the www, and keep them as short as possible:
- `clicksend.com`
- `clicksend.com/contact`
- `clicksend.com/sales`

### Capitalisation
- **ClickSend** — capital C, capital S, no space
- **eCommerce**, **SMS**, **MMS**, **RCS** — capitalised
- Other products lowercase: direct mail, email, SMS marketing

### Oxford comma
No Oxford comma.

### Spelling
Use British English: colour, organisation, specialise, recognised, etc.
